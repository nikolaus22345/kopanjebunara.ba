# Bušenje bunara u BiH — Knowledge Base

Internal reference for the lead-generation business. Everything here is what we need to know to write credible content, price the offer, qualify leads, and not get burned.

Currency note: KM (BAM) is pegged to the euro at **1 EUR = 1.95583 KM**. Roughly, 100 KM ≈ 51 EUR.

---

## 1. What we are actually selling

We are not drillers. We are a **lead broker / marketing front** for drilling crews. That means:

- Our product to the customer is **trust + clarity + speed** (get a real price, a real date, a real crew).
- Our product to the driller is **qualified jobs** they don't have to chase.
- Our moat is **organic search**, because the whole category in BiH is bought on Google and OLX, not on brand.

The single most important strategic fact from the research: **nobody in BiH owns the informational layer of this category.** The existing players are either (a) a generic marketplace with thin content, or (b) single drilling companies with small brochure sites. There is no Bosnian-language site that authoritatively answers "how much does it cost, how deep will I have to go where I live, and do I need a permit." That gap is our entry.

## 2. The trade — technical fundamentals

We need to sound like people who have stood next to a rig. Here is the actual anatomy of the job.

### 2.1 Types of wells (this matters for SEO — people search all three terms)

| Type | Bosnian | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| Dug well | **kopani bunar** | Hand-dug, stone/brick lined, historically to ~60 m. Effectively obsolete — dangerous, slow, shallow, contamination-prone. But **people still search "kopanje bunara"** meaning any well. |
| Driven / jetted well | **pobijeni bunar** ("zabijeni") | Cheap and fast, only viable under ~9 m and only in well-filtering sandy ground. Household garden use only. Low price point, high volume. |
| Drilled well | **bušeni bunar**, "dubinski bunar", "arteški bunar" | The modern product. Steel, galvanised, stainless or PVC cased. Hundreds of metres possible. This is where the money is. |

**Terminology trap worth exploiting:** in BiH "**arteški bunar**" is used colloquially for *any* deep drilled well, even though true artesian (self-flowing, pressurised) conditions are rare. Search volume follows the colloquial usage, not the geology. We should target the colloquial term and *then* correct it in the content — that's exactly the kind of page that earns links and trust.

### 2.2 Drilling methods

- **Rotary with mud flush (rotaciono bušenje s isplakom)** — standard in soft/alluvial ground, sands and gravels.
- **Pneumatic down-the-hole hammer (bušenje pneumatskim udarom / DTH čekić)** — the method for rock and karst. HIDRO-KOP in Banja Luka runs this Ø140–500 mm to 200 m depth. This is what you need in Herzegovina.
- **Core drilling (jezgrovanje)** — investigative, for geotech reports rather than water supply.

Rigs seen in the BiH market: Atlas Copco, Bomag, KLEMM KR 805, Janez 600.

### 2.3 What a finished well actually consists of

Customers think they're buying "a hole." They are buying seven things, and **this is the exact confusion that lets bad contractors undercut on the headline price per metre.** Our content should hammer this.

1. **Bušenje** — the borehole itself. Diameter must be **min. 5 cm larger** than the filter/casing pipe (up to Ø200 mm pipe), and **10 cm larger** for bigger pipes.
2. **Kolona / zaštitna cijev** — casing. PVC, galvanised or stainless steel.
3. **Filter (filterska cijev)** — the slotted section in the water-bearing zone. Open area should exceed **20% of the developed filter surface**. Cheap contractors skimp here first.
4. **Šljunčani zasip (gravel pack)** — graded gravel around the filter, typically **1–4 mm in sand**, **4–8 mm in gravel** formations.
5. **Tampon / zaptivka** — clay or cement seal from the surface down to **0.5 m above the filter**. This is what stops surface contamination reaching the aquifer. Skipping it is the most common serious defect.
6. **Razrada i probno crpljenje (development + pumping test)** — two phases: a **step test at three capacity levels**, then continuous pumping — **min. 36 hours in alluvial ground, min. 168 hours (7 days) in fractured rock**. Almost nobody in the household market does the full protocol; that's a legitimate differentiator.
   - Acceptance criteria after development: turbidity **below 1 NTU**, sand content **under 0.5 ml/m³**.
7. **Oprema — pumpa, hidrofor, elektro, priključak.** Almost always billed separately. Typical household spec: submersible pump **0.75–1.1 kW**, flow **2–3 m³/h**, head **50–60 m**, plus a hydrophore tank of **at least 60 l**.

### 2.4 Water testing

- **Fizičko-hemijska analiza:** roughly **50 € to over 1,000 €** depending on how comprehensive the panel is.
- **Basic bacteriological:** cheap, via the cantonal/entity **Zavod za javno zdravstvo**.
- Content angle: "your well water is not automatically drinkable" is true, useful, scary, and generates a natural upsell.

## 3. Hydrogeology of BiH — this drives depth, price, and risk

This is our biggest content advantage. Depth and success probability vary enormously across BiH, and **no competitor site explains this by region.** A page per region with realistic depth expectations is a genuine service and a keyword magnet.

### 3.1 The three aquifer regimes

**A. Intergranular / alluvial (međuzrnska poroznost)** — the easy money.
- Where: the Sava lowlands and major river valleys — **Posavina, Semberija (Bijeljina), Lijevče polje (Gradiška/Laktaši/Srbac), Brčko, Orašje, Odžak, Modriča, Bosanska Krajina lowlands**, plus terraces along the Bosna, Vrbas, Drina, Una, Sana.
- Depths: typically shallow. This is where the "10–30 m" price ladders in the OLX ads apply.
- Success rate: high. Yields good. Water usually needs iron/manganese treatment.
- Commercially: cheapest per metre, fastest jobs, highest close rate. **Highest-volume lead territory.**

**B. Karst / fissure (karstno-pukotinska)** — high risk, high ticket.
- Where: **Herzegovina** (Mostar, Čapljina, Ljubuški, Široki Brijeg, Trebinje, Nevesinje, Gacko, Bileća), western Bosnia and the Dinaric belt, the karst poljes of eastern Herzegovina.
- Behaviour: water moves in **conduits**, not evenly through the rock. You can drill 150 m and hit nothing 20 m from a well that gives 10 l/s. Karst water is naturally the highest quality in BiH — and the most vulnerable to contamination, because there's no filtration through the rock.
- Depths: deep. 100 m+ routinely; rigs in this market advertise **200–300 m+** capability.
- Note: FBiH classifies "small groundwater bodies" in the Sava basin at a threshold of **>10 l/s** yield.
- Commercially: expensive per metre (rock drilling, DTH hammer, slow), and **this is where "no water, no charge" guarantees actually cost the driller money.** Price the risk in.

**C. Flysch, marl and clay basins (slabo vodopropusne naslage)** — the trap.
- Where: the **Sarajevo basin, Zenica–Doboj basin**, much of central Bosnia hill country, Tuzla basin margins.
- Behaviour: poor yields, perched water, deep drilling for little return.
- Commercially: **the leads most likely to end in an unhappy customer.** We should qualify these hard before passing them on.

### 3.2 Practical qualification rule for our intake form

Ask for: **municipality + nearest river/settlement + what the neighbours' wells did.** Neighbouring well depth is the single best free predictor in this trade, and asking the question makes us look expert on the first phone call.

## 4. Pricing — real BiH market numbers

All figures below are what BiH-market sources actually publish. Ranges are wide because **"cijena po metru" is not a standardised unit** — some quotes are drilling only, some are turnkey.

### 4.1 Published BiH figures

| Source / segment | Price |
|---|---|
| Daibau.ba, medium-category terrain, irrigation well or geoprobe | **60–100 KM per running metre** |
| Banja Luka market, Ø160 mm, turnkey ladder | **≤10 m: 130 KM/m** · **10–20 m: 120 KM/m** · **20–30 m: 110 KM/m** |
| OLX listing, Ø150/210 mm, up to 40 m | **from 30 KM/m**, terrain-dependent (drilling only) |
| Small-diameter driven well ("pet četvrti"), flat terrain | **up to 6 m: 230 KM** · **up to 8 m: 320 KM** · **up to 10 m: 400 KM** (total, not per metre) |
| Mostar (karst), minimum 11 m job | **1,350–2,250 KM** |

### 4.2 Regional calibration (Serbia/Croatia — for sanity-checking, not for quoting)

- 50–150 EUR/m overall; shallow (≤30 m) 40–70 EUR/m; medium (30–80 m) 70–110 EUR/m; deep (80–150 m, hard rock) 100–150 EUR/m.
- Typical complete project: **1,500–4,000 EUR**, taking a few days to 2–3 weeks.
- PVC or steel casing pipe: **8–25 EUR/m** depending on grade.
- Pump: 200 EUR budget models to 1,500 EUR+ industrial with VFD; realistic household budget **350–600 EUR for the pump alone**, plus installation.

> One BiH source (IPIN Institut) quotes a "20 to 15,000 KM per metre" range. Treat that as unreliable/garbled — it does not match any other market data. Don't cite it.

### 4.3 The pricing insight that should shape the whole website

The spread between **30 KM/m and 130 KM/m** is not a market inefficiency — it's a **definition gap**. The 30 KM/m quote is a hole in soft ground with no casing, no gravel pack, no seal, no development, no test. The 130 KM/m is a finished, cased, sealed, developed well.

Customers cannot tell these apart, get burned, and are furious. Even Daibau's own price page warns not to contract on lowest price-per-metre alone, because the contractor will save on installation materials and the works that follow drilling.

**So: our flagship page is a brutally honest price explainer that shows exactly what is and isn't in a quote, with a checklist the customer can take to any contractor.** That page will rank, will get shared, and will convert — because it makes us the only honest actor in the customer's mental model.

### 4.4 Seasonality

Demand is agricultural and weather-driven. Peaks in **spring (pre-season irrigation planning, March–May)** and again in **drought summers (July–August, panic buying)**. Winter is slow — frozen ground, harder access. Plan content publishing to lead the spring peak by 4–6 months, i.e. **publish in autumn to rank by March.**

## 5. Legal & permits

Entity-split, as everything in BiH is. Get this right on the site — it's high-intent search traffic and it builds authority instantly.

### 5.1 Federation BiH

**Governing law:** Zakon o vodama FBiH (Sl. novine FBiH 70/06 and amendments).

**The key exemption — opća upotreba voda (Art. 46):** extracting groundwater from a **well on your own land, for basic household needs** (drinking, hygiene, similar domestic use) counts as *general use* and **does not require a water permit**. The exemption explicitly **excludes irrigation** and any use in technological/economic processes.

**Above that threshold**, you need water acts (*vodni akti*):
- **Prethodna vodna saglasnost** — preliminary consent, at the planning stage.
- **Vodna saglasnost** — consent on project documentation, obtained **before** the building permit.
- **Vodna dozvola** — the operating permit for actual use.

**Issuing bodies:**
- **Agencija za vodno područje rijeke Save, Sarajevo** — voda.ba — Sava basin (most of the Federation). Forms published at voda.ba/obrasci-zahtjeva.
- **Agencija za vodno područje Jadranskog mora, Mostar** — Adriatic basin (Herzegovina).
- **Cantonal ministries** for smaller/local matters; each canton also has its own water law layered on top (e.g. HNK has its own).

**Also relevant — Zakon o geološkim istraživanjima FBiH (Sl. novine FBiH 9/10):** exploratory well drilling is legally a *geological investigation activity*. Companies performing it need approval to perform registered geological activity, and the programme/project and final report must be produced by qualified personnel. Administered by the **Federalno ministarstvo energije, rudarstva i industrije**. There is a Pravilnik setting the conditions companies must meet.

### 5.2 Republika Srpska

**Governing law:** Zakon o vodama RS. Same general-use logic — abstraction of groundwater from a well on your own land for personal/household needs is general use.

**Issuing body:** **JU "Vode Srpske"** (voders.org), Odjeljenje za vodopravne akte.

**Procedure** runs under special procedural rules in the Water Law with subsidiary application of the general administrative procedure act, in four steps: submit request + documentation → completeness check → decision → appeal (with judicial review as final recourse).

**Forms:** VS-1 (water consent on project documentation for construction/reconstruction/demolition), VS-2 (water consent for construction or expansion of water supply sources), plus specialised forms. **Vodna saglasnost is obtained before the building permit and forms part of the building permit application.**

**For commercial / municipal supply in RS, the full stack is:**
1. Detailed hydrogeological study **before** drilling
2. Economic feasibility study
3. **Concession agreement** with the responsible ministry (industry/mining/energy)
4. Final well report with capacity calculations after completion
5. Reserves assessment and **exploitation concession** before abstraction starts

**For domestic household use: no permit required.**

### 5.3 Brčko Distrikt

Separate legal regime again. Verify separately before publishing anything about Brčko.

### 5.4 What this means commercially

- **~90% of our expected leads (household, garden, small farm) fall under the no-permit exemption.** That's a huge relief message and a huge search term: *"da li mi treba dozvola za bunar"*.
- **Farmers irrigating and any business use DO need permits.** That's a real service we can broker as an upsell — permit paperwork is a genuine pain and a margin opportunity.
- **Our partners must be properly registered.** If we push them into commercial jobs they're not licensed for, that's our reputation and potentially our liability.

## 7. Our business model — economics and the one big risk

### 7.1 Take-rate options

| Model | Pro | Con |
|---|---|---|
| **% of job value** | Aligned upside; a 3,000 KM job at 10% = 300 KM | **Requires trusting self-reported job value in a cash trade.** Very leaky. |
| **Fixed fee per qualified lead** | Simple, verifiable, invoiceable, no dependence on job outcome | Partner resents paying for leads that don't close |
| **Monthly retainer per partner per territory** | Predictable revenue, easy accounting, exclusivity is the product | Hard to sell before we have proven volume |

**Recommendation:** start with **per-qualified-lead pricing** (that's also exactly how Daibau monetises — subscription packages, no commission), and move to territory exclusivity retainers once we can show volume. Percentage-of-job sounds better and collects worse.

### 7.2 The disintermediation risk — take this seriously

This trade is **cash, on-site, relationship-based.** The customer and the driller stand in a field together for two days. There is nothing stopping a partner from taking the job off-book, and nothing stopping the customer from calling them directly next time. Structural defences:

- **Own the phone number and the form.** Every lead routes through our tracked number/CRM first. If a partner is invoiced per lead delivered, off-book completion doesn't matter — we already billed.
- **Never hand over the raw lead before it's counted.**
- **Multiple partners per territory** so no single one has leverage over us.
- **Own the brand and the domain**, not the crews. Our asset is the traffic, permanently.

### 7.3 Legal/administrative setup we need

- A registered entity (**d.o.o.** or **obrt**) to invoice commissions legally.
- Clear written agreement with each drilling partner: territory, lead price, response SLA, what happens on a dud lead.
- **Do not present ourselves as the driller if we're not registered for that activity.** Copy should read as "we connect you with verified crews" or we need the operating registration ourselves. This is a consumer-protection and tax exposure, and it's easy to get right at the start and painful to fix later.

## 8. Website and SEO plan

### 8.1 Domains

**Reality check on the multi-domain idea:** buying several exact-match domains and pointing them all at one site is fine **only if one is canonical and the rest 301-redirect to it.** Building *duplicate or near-duplicate sites* across several exact-match domains is a doorway-site pattern, and Google demotes it. It's not worth the risk when the same budget buys us content that actually ranks.

**Do this instead:**
- **One canonical brand domain.** Prefer a brandable name we can defend, not a generic keyword string — the best keyword domain (busenjebunara.ba) is already Papago's.
- **Defensive registrations** of close variants and the `.com`, all 301-redirecting to canonical.
- Register **.ba and/or .com.ba** through **NIC.ba / UTIC** (the national registry). Rules: minimum 3 characters, Latin alphanumerics, hyphen allowed but not at start or end; registrant account must be created as either a legal or natural person and every domain from that account is registered to that entity. Conditions in the Pravilnik must be met — unlike gTLDs, not just anyone can register. **A new UTIC price list took effect 1 January 2026** — check current pricing before budgeting.
- Grab the matching **.com** as well, cheap insurance and useful for email.

### 8.2 Site architecture

### 8.3 The two flagship pages (build these first, they carry everything)

**1. `/cijena-busenja-bunara/` — the honest price page.**
Real BiH ranges (Section 4.1), a table of what's in vs out of a quote, the seven components from Section 2.3, the "why 30 KM/m and 130 KM/m are not the same product" explainer, and a downloadable checklist to take to any contractor. This is the page that makes us the trusted party in the market.

**2. `/dozvola-za-bunar/` — the permits page.**
The FBiH/RS split from Section 5, plainly. The headline answer most people need: *for a household well on your own land, you don't need a permit.* Then exactly when you do (irrigation, business, commercial supply), which body, which form. Nobody in BiH has published this clearly.

### 8.4 Regional pages — where we beat Daibau

Not thin templates. Each region page carries: **the actual aquifer type, realistic depth range, expected difficulty, typical price band, and known water-quality issues.** Priority order follows both search demand and job quality:

**Tier 1 (alluvial, high close rate):** Bijeljina/Semberija · Gradiška–Laktaši–Srbac (Lijevče polje) · Brčko · Orašje–Odžak–Modriča (Posavina) · Banja Luka · Prijedor · Doboj · Derventa–Prnjavor
**Tier 2 (large population, mixed geology):** Tuzla · Živinice · Gračanica · Zenica · Sarajevo · Visoko · Travnik · Bihać · Cazin · Sanski Most
**Tier 3 (karst, high ticket, high risk):** Mostar · Čapljina · Ljubuški · Široki Brijeg · Trebinje · Nevesinje · Gacko · Bileća

### 8.5 Keyword map

**Head:** bušenje bunara · kopanje bunara · bunar za vodu · arteški bunar / arterski bunar · dubinsko bušenje
**Commercial:** bušenje bunara cijena · cijena bušenja bunara po metru · koliko košta bunar · bušenje bunara [grad]
**Informational (our content moat):** da li treba dozvola za bunar · vodna saglasnost bunar · kako pronaći vodu na parceli · koliko duboko treba bušiti · da li je voda iz bunara pitka · analiza vode iz bunara · čišćenje i regeneracija bunara · koja pumpa za bunar
**Adjacent high-ticket:** geotermalne sonde · geosonde · bušenje za toplotnu pumpu · istražno bušenje · piezometri

**Note on rašljanje / radiestezija (dowsing):** it is culturally huge in this market — Geo Kajtaz built a brand partly on an unconventional detection method. It's real search demand. Handle it carefully: cover the topic, be respectful of what customers believe, but don't stake our credibility on claims we can't back. Position hydrogeological desk study + local well records as what we rely on.

### 8.6 Local SEO — an honest constraint

Google Business Profile is the biggest single lever for "[service] near me" queries — **and it requires a real business address or a genuine service area with in-person customer contact.** A pure lead-broker creating GBP listings for cities where it has no presence violates Google's guidelines and gets listings suspended, sometimes taking the whole account.

Clean paths, in order of preference:
1. **Register our entity at a real address** and legitimately operate as a service business from it (one strong GBP, service-area configured).
2. **Help each drilling partner set up and optimise their own GBP** with their consent, and get our site linked from it — real value for them, real referral traffic for us.
3. Do **not** fabricate city listings. It's the fastest way to lose the asset.

Because of this constraint, **our ranking strategy has to lean on organic regional pages and content authority rather than the map pack.** That's another reason the content moat matters more here than it would for a normal local service business.

### 8.7 Conversion essentials for this specific market

- **Phone and Viber/WhatsApp above the fold.** This customer calls; they do not fill in long forms.
- Intake form must capture: **općina/opština, nearest settlement or river, intended use (household / garden / irrigation / business), plot access for a truck, and neighbours' well depth if known.**
- **Real photos of real rigs on real Bosnian sites.** Stock photography kills trust instantly in this trade.
- Show **completed job records** — location, depth, date, yield. Papago does this well and it's persuasive.
- Publish a **written price band**, not "contact for quote." Being the only one with a number on the page is most of the battle.

## 9. Content calendar logic

Demand peaks in spring. Google takes months to rank new content. Therefore: **write and publish the flagship pages and Tier 1 regional pages in autumn**, supporting blog content through winter, so we're ranking when the March–May buying season opens. Publishing money pages in April is publishing them a year late.

## 10. Open questions to verify before publishing legal or price claims

These are things I could not fully confirm from public sources and we should nail down before they go on a live site:

1. **Exact FBiH article numbers and current consolidated text** of Zakon o vodama on general use and the permit triggers — the official PDF wouldn't parse cleanly. Get the consolidated text and confirm article numbering.
2. **Whether a specific volumetric threshold (m³/day or l/s) exists in FBiH** that flips a household well into permit territory, or whether it's purely purpose-based ("basic household needs"). Sources suggest purpose-based; confirm with Agencija za vodno područje rijeke Save.
3. **Brčko Distrikt regime** — entirely unverified.
4. **Current NIC.ba/UTIC .ba pricing** under the price list effective 1 January 2026.
5. **Real search volumes.** I have no keyword-volume data — everything in Section 8.5 is inferred from search results, competitor targeting and market logic. Before committing the content budget, run the list through a real keyword tool with BA geo-targeting (Ahrefs/Semrush, or Google Keyword Planner with a live ads account). Expect low absolute volumes and high intent — that's normal and fine for this market size.
6. **Our partners' actual capabilities**, in writing: max depth, diameters, rock vs alluvial capability, rigs owned, territory radius, licences held, and whether they will honour a "no water, no charge" guarantee. Everything customer-facing depends on this and it's the one input only we can get.
